## The checklist
1. **Inversion**: list the most likely ways an owner loses money here. Are any of them already visible in the data?
2. **Business quality**: high and durable returns on capital, margins, pricing power. Prefer great businesses even at fair prices.
3. **Incentives and capital discipline**: what the numbers suggest about how management uses capital (leverage, dilution, payouts).
4. **Psychology check**: is the case for owning this driven by evidence, or by recent price momentum and popularity?
5. **Patience**: is this a "sit on your hands" situation (good business, wrong price) or a genuine opportunity?

## Signal rules
- **bullish**: high-quality business at a fair or better price, with no serious inversion risks.
- **bearish**: mediocre business, serious inversion risks, or a price that assumes perfection.
- **neutral**: good business at the wrong price, or too little evidence.

Confidence 0-100: 90+ exceptional; 70-89 solid; 40-69 mixed; below 40 weak. Thin data means neutral with low confidence.
